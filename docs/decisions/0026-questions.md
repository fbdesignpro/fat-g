# 0026 — Questions: what a session asks is answered from its card

> **Rule:** a question a session asks the user — Claude's `AskUserQuestion`, Codex's blocking `item/tool/requestUserInput`, or the question Codex asks in a message — is a card in the chat at its place, with its options, a field for something else where it takes free text, and one Submit; the first single-choice pick on the last question may also submit under item 12's first-visit rule. A blocking question raises the session's attention and holds its turn until it is answered, withdrawn or stopped; an async one waits for nothing, and is passed over once a later message is sent or its model went on past it in a turn that has ended. The answer goes to the CLI by the one route its provider has, and grants no action. The question, its answers and what became of it are an item of the conversation, kept in the conversation store alone until the user removes it; a secret answer is sent and never kept. One registry in main holds every live request, in memory, and nothing pending survives a restart. fat-g has no timeout and no default answer of its own.

- **Status:** Accepted by the maintainer with M21's final spec (spec sheet (private): S1–S9, C4–C79), from the chat roadmap's M21 outline and the chat decisions on questions. Implemented in M21. M22, amended items 5 and 14 ([0027](0027-execution-permissions.md)): the registry holds approvals beside questions, and a request to act is a card of its own kind. The maintainer's acceptance with the installed CLIs (M21, V15), is recorded in the milestone document. M100b, redrew the card as a panel — a header counting the request's questions, one question at a time, numbered rows picked by the mouse or a key — counted the attention by questions rather than requests, and amended items 4, 8, 12 and 15: a question left blank is sent as "No answer", and Decline declines a whole request. M22b, amended item 5 and the interaction's kinds (C10, C12, C17, C27, C56; [0029](0029-plans.md)): a third body kind, a plan's line; Claude's `ExitPlanMode` request is held in the registry under the `plan` kind, and a Codex plan's or a brought plan's decision lives on its line in the store, outside the registry, across a restart.

  M23 (C29, C45): an async card's answer, while its turn runs and takes a message, goes into that turn as a message sent now — the card answered in that message's own transaction — and otherwise as a prompt, as before; a blocking card keeps its direct reply.

## Context

- A model that needs the user's word asks for it. Before M21 fat-g refused the asking: Claude's question tool was not offered, so the model guessed or asked in prose; Codex's blocking request would have got "method not found"; and a question Codex asked in a message was drawn as plain lines, to be answered by typing it out. M14 had built the place for it — a session's attention, "1 QUESTION" on its capsule, `asking` on its action line — and M18 had reserved the `interaction` kind, and nothing filled either.
- The two CLIs ask in three ways, traced under the app's own launches (chat-protocols.md §14 (private research note), M21's K1 and K2). Claude Code sends a `can_use_tool` request for `AskUserQuestion` and holds the turn; it is answered `allow` with `updatedInput.answers`, a map from each question's whole text to one string; a Stop takes the request back with `control_cancel_request`; a killed process leaves nothing pending to resume; a sub-agent is offered no question tool. Codex's app-server sends `item/tool/requestUserInput` in a plan turn — which needs the experimental API, so the app does not reach it before M22b — answered with `answers` keyed by each question's id, and resolved by `serverRequest/resolved`; in default mode it asks in an `agentMessage` with `questions`, ends its turn, and takes the answer as a later prompt, the message kept in its history with its questions.
- "Async", "optional", "ephemeral" and "expired" are different properties of a request, and no provider gives a timeout fat-g could show honestly.
- Codex later offers its model `request_user_input_async`, in default and plan modes alike, which "returns immediately without ending the turn or waiting for a reply" (chat-protocols.md (private research note), §22): the async question then comes in the middle of a turn, and the model goes on, taking its own first option, listed as its recommendation, as the working answer. A card left open after such a turn asks for what the model already settled without it, and nothing says so.
- A request must be replied to: one left unanswered holds the CLI's turn for ever.

## Decision

1. **Two modes.** `blocking`: the CLI made a request and holds the turn, or a worker, until it is answered or withdrawn; the session's attention is raised, and its action line reads "Asking a question" in fat-g's words, never the question's. `async`: the CLI asked in a message and went on or ended the turn; no attention is raised beyond the turn's own end. A provider declares which it has — `none`, `blocking`, `async` or `both`: Claude `blocking`, Codex `both` — and one declaring `none` raises no card.
2. **A question is an item of the conversation.** Its kind is `interaction`, M18's reserved kind given its body ([0019](0019-conversation-store.md), item 5): its mode, its questions — each its text, a header, its options with their descriptions, whether several may be chosen, whether free text is taken, whether the answer is a secret — its answers, its outcome, and when each was set. It stands where the question was asked, once for its source, and is the session's own or a worker's. It is bounded on the way in: 16 questions, 8 options, 500 units for a text, a header or a label, 2,000 for a description, cut past them with a flag the card shows; an answer's text is bounded at 10,000 units and refused past it, never cut. *(Amended: the message whose questions a card of the list draws is not drawn — its text is the CLI's own copy of those questions, and the card stands for it; a message whose card the list does not hold, and an aside's or a worker's, keep their lines.)*
3. **A blocking request that cannot be drawn whole is refused, not cut.** More than 16 questions, a question with more than 8 options that takes no free text, or one with no option that takes no free text: the adapter refuses it on the wire as it refuses what fat-g cannot show, with the notice, and no card is made. Cut, the card would ask less than the CLI's reply must name.
4. **Seven outcomes, none inferred from time.** `pending` and `open` while it waits; `answered`; `dismissed`, an async card the user folded away, the CLI told nothing *(M100b: or a blocking one the user declined, its request declined to the CLI — "Declined" in fat-g's words)*; `passed`, an async question a later message went by *(amended: or that its model went on past in a turn that has ended, item 11)*; `withdrawn`, a request the CLI took back or whose turn or worker ended without an answer; `dropped`, a request pending when its process was lost, fat-g quit or started again, or one answered whose answer the process could not take. There is no `expired`, no timeout and no default answer.
5. **One registry in main.** Every live request of every session is held in memory, keyed by its session, the process generation it came on and the provider's request id, with its owner — a turn or a worker — and the item it opened. A repeated delivery makes one card; a request main cannot place is refused through the session, so nothing the CLI asked waits on nothing; an answer is taken only for a request held pending on the session's current process, and only if it fits its questions. An answer and a withdrawal race by their arrival, and the first settles the item. A request lives as long as its owner: a worker's outlives its turn while the worker runs. M22's approvals and M22b's plans take the same registry. *(M22, [0027](0027-execution-permissions.md), item 15: approvals are held there since, keyed by a lossless request identity; a request that comes again under a held id with another payload is refused and its card withdrawn as changed, a question's too.)* *(M22b, C10, C12, C56; [0029](0029-plans.md), item 6:)* the registry holds Claude's `ExitPlanMode` request under the `plan` kind, and the plan's line is pending exactly while it is: a decision is taken only while it is pending on the session's current process. A plan no request holds — Codex's, or one brought into a chat — raises nothing in the registry: its decision is its line's in the store, taken once, and it waits across a restart.
6. **The answer is on disk before it is sent, and final on fat-g's side.** A blocking answer is written in one durable transaction, then given to the CLI; whatever the CLI does next, the card reads answered. One the process could not take — the write refused, the process gone — reads `dropped`, and nothing sends an answer again. The CLI's own confirmation that it took the answer marks the item; one without the mark says so only once what carries the answer is over, and is never relabelled by an end.
7. **A loss drops; an end withdraws.** Each adapter says a request it still held unanswered was dropped before the ends it makes for a lost process, and the runtime drops before anything it ends itself, so a question pending at a crash, a quit or a close reads "Not answered: the session’s process ended" and never "Withdrawn". A card an earlier run left pending reads dropped at the next launch, and is never answered: no provider's list of pending requests is read.
8. **On Claude: a third answer, for one tool's name.** The launch's tool list is five, `AskUserQuestion` the fifth, a sub-agent's the same ([0020](0020-claude-chat-sessions.md), items 4 and 5): it reads nothing, writes nothing and reaches nothing. Its request is answered `allow` with `updatedInput` — the request's own `input` as it came, with `answers` keyed by each question's whole text, several options and a typed text joined by a comma and a space — for that tool's name and no other ([0020](0020-claude-chat-sessions.md), item 3). Every other `can_use_tool` is still denied. *(M100b: a question the user skipped is answered `deny` in their name, with the words "The user declined to answer in fat-g"; its spike saw the turn go on.)* Its `tool_use` makes no activity ([0024](0024-a-sessions-work.md), item 1): the card stands for it. A Stop sends `interrupt` as before.
9. **On Codex: a reply to a request, and a prompt.** `item/tool/requestUserInput` is held and answered when the answer comes, with `answers` keyed by each question's id, the chosen options and the typed text as separate strings ([0018](0018-codex-sessions.md), item 7): a reply to a request the CLI made, and no method of fat-g's. The transport lets a handler answer later, its owner writing the reply and learning whether the line went. An async question is answered by a prompt of the session's in fat-g's words — each question's title on a line and its answer on the next — sent as any prompt; its card's answer, the prompt's record and the pass of the other open cards are one transaction. The history's message with questions makes a card too, `passed` where any turn follows it. The empty message Codex sends after an async question makes no item. The app sends no plan turn and asks for no experimental API: the blocking card goes live in the app with M22b.
10. **An answer is encoded from the request as it came.** The adapter keeps the raw request, in memory, until it is answered or abandoned, and puts the provider's own key and each option's whole label at encoding. The card, the store and the registry hold the bounded questions and the places of what was chosen: a label cut for display may no longer tell two options apart.
11. **A card passes when the conversation moves on.** An open async card is passed by whatever comes after it — a prompt, an answer to another card, a turn the history check brings in from the provider's own app — since the model has moved on with the newer message. The user can type the answer again.

    *(Amended:)* and by its own turn's end, where the model went on past it: a message of the session's own with text, or a plan's line, stands after it in its turn. It is passed once that turn's end is written and the items the end came with are in, before a review or a plan's workflow reads whether the turn ended by asking; the history check passes it in an ended turn it brings in or completes. A card that is its turn's last word — a sub-agent's message, an activity or an empty message after it says nothing — stays open: the model ended its turn on it. While its turn runs the card waits as before, and its answer goes into the turn. Its words are "Passed over: the conversation went on without an answer", one wording for both ends, so the stored item says nothing new.
12. **Nothing is chosen for the user.** The card's controls stand in no form; no option is selected at first. Submit is available once every question has been shown, and needs at least one answer; a question left blank is sent as "No answer" by each provider's route (M100b). Decline declines the whole request where the provider declares `declines`, and folds an async card away. Once settled a card takes no further answer and stays, its outcome in words and its answers as text. An answered card starts folded under its header, whose left arrow shows or hides its questions and recorded answers. This presentation state is local to the mounted card and never saved; an unconfirmed-answer notice stays visible while folded. Other settled outcomes keep their full text visible.

    A first single-choice answer on the last question may submit after 200 ms when picked by the pointer or its number key, only on that question's first visit and only if every earlier question was already answered on arrival and every question is still answered when the delay ends. A one-question card starts eligible. Leaving the last question or answering it consumes this chance; revising an answer, including free text, cancels a pending automatic action, and returning never restores eligibility. Navigation cancels pending automatic actions too. Multiple-choice toggles, free-text entry, Space and arrow-key picks never auto-submit. Submit remains available under the existing rules for revisions and skipped answers. This narrows M100b's C60 at the maintainer's request.
13. **A secret answer is sent and not kept.** Its field masks what is typed; its text crosses main to the CLI and is written nowhere; the item keeps the fact that one was given.
14. **An answer grants nothing.** It is the reply to a question, and no approval: every other request either CLI makes is refused as before ([0018](0018-codex-sessions.md), item 7; [0020](0020-claude-chat-sessions.md), item 3). *(M22, [0027](0027-execution-permissions.md): a request to run a command, change a file or use a tool is now a card of its own kind under a mode that asks, decided through `chat:decide`; an answer to a question still grants nothing, and the two share the registry and nothing else.)*
15. **In the store and the Main window alone.** Two channels, `chat:answer` and `chat:dismiss`, the Main window's only *(M100b: `chat:dismiss` declines a blocking card through the session's `decline`)*; the page names the session and the card's item, never a request. A question, its options and an answer reach no log, no summary, no app state and never the Pet: main says kinds and counts.
16. **Every outcome is an event.** The agent events are at version 7: `question-raised`, with the request's id, the id of what carried it and its questions, bounded; and `question-resolved`, by a reason — `withdrawn`, `dropped` or `confirmed`. A session takes an answer through `answer`, present exactly where its provider declares blocking questions.

## What is left out

- Approvals, remembered rules and any tool that writes, runs a command or reaches the network (M22); plan mode, and so Codex's blocking question live in the app (M22b); an answer sent while a turn runs, and attachments in an answer (M23).
- A card for a question asked in an aside or by a sub-agent in a message: those messages keep their plain lines, since no prompt reaches either.
- MCP elicitation forms, Claude's `request_user_dialog` and interactive MCP apps: refused as before, with the notice (M22c for the first). ** Codex's leave to run an MCP tool, which Codex asks as an elicitation, is no form: it is a request for leave ([0018](0018-codex-sessions.md), item 7).
- A timeout or a default answer of fat-g's own, a question answered from the Pet ([0010](0010-pet-deferred.md)), and a search over questions.

## Consequences

- Claude's launch now offers the model a way to ask, and the user's own configuration or a later CLI may change what it asks: the bounds cut what can be cut, the adapter refuses what cannot be drawn whole, and the spike that traced it runs again at each new CLI version (M21, B1).
- `conversations.sqlite` now holds the questions a session asked and the answers given, with the chat. [PRIVACY.md](../../PRIVACY.md) says so, and that a secret answer is sent and not kept. The leak scans take the captures' question texts, options and answers as needles.
- The store is at version 5: the `interaction` kind has its body, under a key of its own, and no column is added. No migration: a version-4 store is refused as older ([0014](0014-format-compatibility-starts-at-first-release.md)).
- An answer the CLI never confirmed is said to be so, and no more: fat-g cannot know what the model did with it.
- A review's or a plan's turn that asked during its work and went on is read by what it wrote, not held on its card (item 11). A card an earlier build left open after such a turn stays open: nothing passes it but a later message or the user's Decline. The option Codex lists first as its recommendation is not marked on the card.
- Codex's blocking path ships tested against its capture through the replay fake alone; M22b's acceptance is its first live run.
- Whether Codex's question in a message is a card is the model's doing: the card is made from the message's own `questions`, and never from its words. A model that writes its question as prose — GPT-6-Sol at high effort did, at the acceptance, where gpt-6-astra at low effort sent `questions` — gets no card, and is answered by typing.
- A new provider declares `questions`, sends `question-raised` for a request it can place, takes `answer`, and sends `question-resolved`; `tests/sessions/facet-contract.ts` holds it to an answer that completes, a request main refuses and one a Stop leaves to its turn's end, and nothing in the shared code names a provider.

## Revisit when

- Either CLI gives a request a timeout, or a default answer, that the user should see: item 4.
- Claude Code offers the question tool to a sub-agent: item 8's worker path, built and unobserved, is then live.
- M22 adds approvals to the registry: item 5 gains a kind, and item 14 is restated there. *(M22 did: [0027](0027-execution-permissions.md), items 11 and 15 — the registry holds both kinds, and a request's resolution gained `changed`.)*
- M22b sends plan turns: item 9's blocking card goes live in the app, and whether the app asks for the experimental API is decided there — and how Codex is told a blocking question was skipped, its card having no Decline until then (M100b, C33).
- M23 adds an answer while a turn runs: an async card's Submit, disabled while one does, takes the busy route.
- A provider's history returns a blocking question: item 7's "what fat-g recorded is all there is" would have a second source.
- Codex turns on its blocking question outside plan mode (`default_mode_request_user_input`) or its message for the user's attention (`send_message_to_user_async`), or a model asks during its work and then ends its turn waiting on the answer in words: item 11's "went on past it".
