# 0031 — Avatars: two roots, one format, one source

> **Rule:** an avatar is a folder of image files named by their role, in a family's folder, in one of two roots of one layout — fat-g's own art beside its program, and the `avatars/` folder the user fills — read by one scanner in main, and drawn only through `app://media/avatars/<id>`. A family is its folder: its name is the folder's, its avatars what the scan finds, and its look and matches its own `family.json`, the file its editor writes there; Make a copy can also create a new user family from built-in art, and ** Add avatar a new avatar from an image the page scaled ([0036](0036-adding-avatars.md)).
>
> - **The format:** `<root>/<Family>/<Avatar>/portrait.(png|webp)`, required, and `small.(png|webp)`, optional, role names matched without regard to ASCII case; a family may hold `family.json` and `LICENSE.txt`. A portrait and a small are square still PNG or WebP images within the one image bound, 5 MiB and 8,000 px a side, judged by their header alone with the one header reader fat-g has.
> - **The two roots:** `resources/avatars/` in a package (`assets/avatars/` in a development run), and `avatars/` in the data folder, which fat-g creates at Show avatars folder or Make a copy. A user family named like a built-in one is a family of its own, never merged or shadowing. No link is followed, no dot folder entered, and a root's scan stops at 2,000 entries.
> - **The manifest:** `family.json`, versioned as every stored format is (version 7): a provider (`any` by default, or one provider id), an author, a licence, the avatars in the author's order with their match entries, the `Default` folder apart — the family default, whose look is the family's own and which a model nothing matches shows — the providers whose starting family it is (`defaultFor`, read for fat-g's own families alone), and the overlay's two styles, the family's and each avatar's ([0032](0032-avatar-overlay.md)). Unknown fields are skipped as it is read and kept as it is written; a manifest that cannot be used is refused whole with its cause, the family stands as its folders, and the file is never written; an overlay field of the wrong form is dropped alone. ** A `name` is no longer read: the folder names the family.
> - **The writes:** in a root fat-g may write — the user's, and fat-g's own art only in a development run, where it is the repository's `assets/avatars/` — a scan makes the `family.json` a family holding an avatar lacks, with fat-g's defaults, and the family editor's Save family replaces it whole, temp file then rename, from a draft checked against the scan and the file. **Make a copy** also creates a new user family from a built-in one, its checked role images, manifest and optional licence copied unchanged, with no link or unrelated file followed. Its name is **Copy of ...**, then **(2)** and so on without overwriting anything. A failed copy removes only its own reserved folder. **Add avatar** ([0036](0036-adding-avatars.md)) creates a new avatar's folder in a family, its two PNGs and its entry in `family.json`. **Create family** ([0036](0036-adding-avatars.md), item 7) creates a new user family from those PNGs, its required `Default` and an optional matched avatar, without overwriting any existing family. An installed copy writes nothing beside its program.
> - **The catalog:** both roots scanned at start and at each Rescan into one catalog in memory, nothing watched and nothing stored. An id is opaque and the same for an unchanged file; a provider's starting family is the first built-in family, by name, whose `defaultFor` names it, its built-in face that family's `Default` folder, and that family is the provider's active family until the user chooses another.
> - **The source:** `app://media/avatars/<id>` answers an id the current catalog minted, after checking the file again — a regular file and no link, of the size the scan saw, its real path inside its root's, its header still the same — with `cache-control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable`; the route, its parsing and its policy are 0030's.
> - **The active family and the overrides:** each provider has an active family — the user's choice in the preferences, else its starting family — whose match entries are searched each time a model is resolved, and never written. The user's assignment of one avatar to one model, by its reference — its root's origin and its two folders' names — global to the provider, is an override that stands before the search; one avatar may be several models' override, and the search may give one avatar to several models. Nothing automatic changes an assignment. Main resolves each session and catalog model to its override, then its active family's strongest match, then that family's face, then the provider's built-in face, then none — and each sub-agent a session entry lists, by the identifiers of the model it runs on, as its session is; a reference the scan does not list, or whose avatar has no portrait, resolves past itself.
> - **What never holds it:** `records.json`, the conversation store, a log (counts alone), the Pet's projection — but for a listed session's file ids and overlay ([0010](0010-pet-deferred.md)) — and a project's folders; the preferences hold references and family names, and nothing of the art or its look.
> - **The identifier:** the user chooses a CLI key that follows the CLI, or a resolved model ID that stays with one version. The version-ID override takes priority over the key's; the specific-avatar override dialog shows a model by its name, while Accounts shows name and ID, and a model's override and overlay are kept under the identifier chosen. See Version identifiers below.

## Context

- **A face for each model.** The final look draws an avatar wherever a session or a model stands, at four sizes from 16 to 176 px; until M102 each was the provider's coloured square.
- **Art is files.** The art fat-g ships and the art a user adds should be one format, so that a family moves between them unchanged, and adding one is adding a folder (decision 0002's spirit for assets).
- **The page may load no file.** The renderer has no file system, and 0021 lets no message load an image: an avatar must come from main, by a route that names no path. M23 built that route for attachments.

## Decision

1. **Folders and roles, not a registry.** An avatar is its folder; its files are found by their names. Nothing outside the folders lists them, so a family is complete when its folder is.
2. **Judged by header.** Each role file is checked without decoding, by the reader the attachments share: its signature must agree with its extension, its pixels and bytes be within the one bound, its sides equal, its animation absent. The scan reads an image's first 64 KiB, and the whole of a PNG whose chunks reach no `IDAT` within them. A file that fails is listed with its problem and used nowhere; two files for one role are used by neither; an avatar without a usable portrait is drawn nowhere.
3. **One scanner, one writer, one adapter.** The scanner, the manifest reader, the writer of `family.json` and the source reach the file system through one adapter, which writes only the user's empty folder at Show avatars folder and a family's `family.json` (item 10), Make a copy's new family, and ** Add avatar's new avatar ([0036](0036-adding-avatars.md)). They are Electron-free, and the architecture tests pin the adapter as the area's one reader and one writer.
4. **Ids, not paths.** A file's id is the first 32 hexadecimal digits of a SHA-256 of its root, its path in it, its size and its time. An unchanged file keeps its id across a Rescan and a restart; a changed one gets a new one; an id the current catalog did not mint is refused.
5. **Cached by Chromium, under the id.** An avatar is read and decoded once per run and may be kept in Chromium's cache in the data folder across restarts, since its id changes with its file. An attachment stays `no-store` (0030).
6. **Resolved in main.** Each session entry and catalog model carries the avatar it draws, resolved by main, and why: its model's override (item 8), then its provider's active family searched (item 9), then that family's default, then its provider's built-in face, then none. ** With it comes its overlay ([0032](0032-avatar-overlay.md)). A session is resolved by the key of its model in force, found as the model column finds it — by key, then by resolved id, within its own account's list, and the id it reports where the catalog has none — so a session held, or on an account no longer configured, still resolves by its provider's. *(M106)* Each sub-agent a session entry lists is resolved the same way by the key of the model it runs on, within its session's account, once a model each time the entry is made; an alias no catalog lists falls to the face until the id comes, and a sub-agent whose model no frame named has none and draws its session's. The Pet's projection holds none of it but, *([0010](0010-pet-deferred.md))* for each session it lists, the files its avatar draws and its overlay — never the reference, the reason, a family's name or the listing. Resolution runs again on each change of the preferences' avatar section, the catalog and the sessions, and a resolution that draws the same — the same files, for the same reason, under the same overlay — keeps the object a session entry held, sent to no window again. The page draws a chain over the placeholder — the small at 36 px and less (the Pet's smaller capsules), else the portrait, else the provider's square — and a file that fails to draw falls to the next step, remembered by its id.
7. **Built-in art in the package.** `assets/avatars/` is copied beside the archive as `resources/avatars/`, outside `app.asar`, and audited by its own rules (0007): only family manifests, licences and role files; a family naming a licence must hold its `LICENSE.txt` and its line in the notices; 4 MiB in all; every file read by the privacy scan.

8. **Overrides in the preferences (M103).** The assignments are a section of the preferences (revision 9, [0005](0005-preferences-compatibility.md); revision 14): by provider id, then by CLI key or resolved version ID, a reference of the avatar's root's origin and its two folders' names as listed. An override is the provider's, shared by all its accounts, and a key need not be listed by any of them to hold one. One avatar may be the override of several models, of one provider or several: a patch sets or clears one key and touches no other. Nothing automatic changes an override — not a scan, a report that drops the model, a folder renamed or removed, nor a Rescan — so a renamed folder is a new avatar: the model falls back to its active family, its row says "Not found", and the override comes back if the folder's name does. The page sends main one patch of one provider's entries — Assign avatar or Clear — by the preferences' own channel, and nothing else.

A family's `provider` limits its family and individual avatar choices to that provider; `any` offers it to every provider. Its active-family search, default and built-in starting-family selection follow the same scope. Changing the scope leaves explicit model overrides untouched and falls back from an incompatible active family. The family editor edits Provider below Family default and saves it in `family.json`; new files start with `any`, and the four shipped families name their respective providers. Both manifest version fields are 7, with no migration or compatibility reader.

9. **Searched by the active family (M104's pairing retired).** Each provider's active family is the family the user chose for it (`avatars.active`, revision 14), where the scan lists it and it can stand — its `Default` folder has a usable portrait — else the provider's starting family, the first built-in family by name whose `defaultFor` names it and that can stand; Settings → Avatars chooses it, the default named, and choosing the default stores none, so it follows the art fat-g ships. A model with no override takes the family's avatar whose entry matches it most strongly: an entry matches a model by the whole of its key or of its resolved id, ASCII case aside, `*` standing for any run of characters, an entry qualified with another provider's id taking no part; an exact key is the stronger over a pattern, then the one with more characters other than `*`, then a qualified one over an unqualified one, and at equal strength the avatar first in the family's order. Several models may take one avatar. Where nothing matches, the family's default — its `Default` folder, whatever the provider; a user family's too, once chosen — then the provider's built-in face, its starting family's default. The search is made at each resolution, so a model a CLI lists tomorrow is drawn by the family at once, and its result is never written. Assign family, which wrote a family's pairing as one override per model, is retired with its pairing and its one-avatar-one-model rule.

10. **Families as files.** A family is its folder, named by it; its avatars are the folders the scan finds in it; and how it draws and what it matches are its own `family.json`, so a family copied to another installation draws as it did, with nothing of its look in the preferences.
    - **Made where missing.** A scan of a root fat-g may write gives a family folder holding an avatar and no `family.json` one at version 7, with fat-g's defaults written out whole — the version's look and the effort marks' — and its avatars' folders in name order with no match. A file already there under any case of its name, a link, or a file fat-g refuses is left as it is; the family stands as its folders.
    - **Edited as a draft.** Settings → Avatars' family editor opens a family by its reference (`avatars:family`): main reads its file again and answers what it holds, its revision — the SHA-256 of its bytes — and whether it can be saved; fat-g's own art in an installed copy, a link or a refused file opens read-only, saying why. The editor changes the Default style — the `Default` folder's, every other avatar's starting point — and each other avatar's own, their match entries, and drops the entry of an avatar whose folder is gone; it leaves `defaultFor` as the file holds it, and creates, renames and deletes no file but Add avatar's new folder and its two files, and an avatar's image replaced, the old file sent to the system's trash *([0036](0036-adding-avatars.md))*, each written at once like Make a copy's.
    - **Saved whole.** Save family (`avatars:saveFamily`) sends the draft and the revision it was made from. Main finds the family by the last scan, never by a path the page names; checks its folder is no link and lies one name inside its root's real path, and that the file is no link; reads the file again and refuses a save made from another revision as a conflict, writing nothing; checks each folder of the draft is one the scan lists or the file names, once, and each face a listed avatar of a registered provider. It then writes the file — the version pair set, the known fields the draft's, every other field kept where the file held it, the faces of providers this build lacks kept — beside it as a temp file renamed over it, and scans both roots again before it answers. A save and a scan never run at once.
    - **Make a copy.** The installed built-in editor says "Built-in avatar families cannot be changed. But you can Make a copy and edit it.", with **Make a copy** an inline button. Main accepts a built-in reference alone, checks the source paths and images again, preserves manifest bytes (including unknown fields) and the licence, reserves a fresh user-family folder exclusively, then scans and returns its reference and listing. The editor opens the new family immediately; no rescan or folder-manager step. The copy remains if editing is cancelled; choosing it as a provider's active family is separate. The development editor also offers Make a copy beside Cancel while keeping direct edits available, and asks before discarding unsaved edits to copy the saved family. Copies, saves and scans are serialized.
    - **fat-g's own art.** A development run writes the repository's `assets/avatars/` as it does the user's folder: that is how maintainers edit the families fat-g ships ([`docs/maintaining-avatar-art.md`](../maintaining-avatar-art.md)). A smoke or stream run and an installed copy never write it.

## Version identifiers

An assignment's model identifier may be the CLI key or the resolved version ID, both already valid identifiers in the revision-9 dictionary. Settings → Accounts shows the model name and resolved ID; the specific-avatar override dialog shows the name alone (the key and ID line removed at the maintainer's word). Ordinary model labels use the name, including its version when supplied by the CLI. The screen's **Assign by** choice selects **Follow CLI key** or **This version (ID)** for a model's override and its version style; the choice itself writes nothing. A version ID is preferred to a display name, which a catalog override may rename. With no reported ID, the key is used.

Main resolves a model's version-ID override first, then its key override if no version override exists, then its active family (item 9). A present reference with unusable art still falls past itself. Resolution changes no overrides. A saved ID stays with that version when an alias moves, across accounts, and when the catalog stops listing it; a saved alias continues to follow the CLI. Family matching checks both the key and the resolved ID. The stored shape and accepted identifier alphabet were unchanged then, so there was no format revision or migration.

Evidence: model-only discovery on Claude Code 2.1.302 and codex-cli 0.164.0, with no turns; Claude reports `opus` → `claude-opus-5-5` and `fable` → `claude-fable-5-1`. Regression tests cover an alias moving to a new version, resolution by either identifier, a family saved by ID, and the account list's ID column.

## Consequences

- A user's family is drawn on a session once one of its avatars is the session's model's override (M103), or once it is chosen as the provider's family; the tab says which families lack a `Default` folder, and so are not used.
- A family the user drops into `avatars/` is given its `family.json` at the next Rescan and is edited from the tab from then on; a family copied elsewhere takes its look and its matches with it.
- A family whose manifest names the models its avatars suit draws them on every model it matches with no press at all, models listed later included; a manifest's `matches` are searched at resolution and never written as overrides.
- Shipping art "pre-assigned" is shipping a family whose matches suit the CLIs' names: the built-in family whose `defaultFor` names a provider is its active family until the user chooses another.
- A reference is folder names, the built-in art's or the user's own: renaming a family or an avatar loses its assignments, and moving a family to the other root makes it another avatar.
- A Rescan is the only way fat-g learns of a change in the folders; the tab and every drawn avatar follow it.
- Chromium's cache may hold a drawn avatar's bytes in the data folder; `PRIVACY.md` says so, and deleting the data folder removes them.
- The built-in art is a placeholder in M102, the maintainer's own under fat-g's MIT licence, crediting nothing; a family with a licence of its own is credited by its manifest alone.

## Conversation speaker projection

Main resolves the avatar for every model a conversation names, including models absent from its CLI catalog and stored sub-agents absent from the current session summary, through the same resolver. Successful chat, page, aside and worker reads, and item pushes, carry transient presentation metadata keyed by the model's reported identifier; worker reads also carry the worker's own model identity, or its session's as assumed when none was recorded. These are not fields of stored conversation items.

Main remembers only the projected model and worker identifiers for the selected session, clearing them when selection changes or the session is removed. Changes of the avatar preferences, the catalog and the scan refresh them through presentation-only `chat:message` pushes to Main alone. Presentation revisions are independent of transcript revisions: a refresh neither rereads conversation text nor moves its window, and an older presentation response cannot overwrite a newer one. A missing override falls through to the active family, its face and the built-in face, then the existing placeholder. Nothing of this projection reaches records, the conversation store, a log or the Pet.

## Revisit when

- Avatars move (M105's loops) or need video: the format gains roles, and the route Range.
- A family needs derived copies (scaled smalls) made by fat-g: that would be a decode, which this record rules out in main. ** [0036](0036-adding-avatars.md) makes them for an added image, decoded and scaled in the page alone.
- A user's folder outgrows 2,000 entries in normal use.

## Explicit full-data reset

[Decision 0038](0038-full-data-reset.md) adds a separate, twice-confirmed removal of all data in the current installation's data folder, followed by a normal quit and cleanup before the next startup. This explicit operation removes even unreadable files, backups, catalog overrides and user media; the ordinary read-only, retention and set-aside behavior in this record is unchanged outside that reset. No project or CLI folder is reached.
